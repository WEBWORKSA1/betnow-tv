Elo Ratings Explained: From Chess to the NBA

By the BetNow.tv Analytics Desk · 6 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

Arpad Elo designed his rating system for chess in the 1960s. Today variants power rankings in soccer, American football, basketball, esports and more, because the core idea is elegant: every result moves ratings by an amount proportional to how surprising it was.

The win-probability formula

For teams A and B with ratings RA and RB:

P(A wins) = 1 ÷ (1 + 10(RB − RA) / 400)

A 100-point gap gives the stronger side about 64%; 200 points ≈ 76%; 400 points ≈ 91%.

Updating after a game

New rating = old rating + K × (actual result − expected result), where the result is 1 for a win, 0.5 for a draw and 0 for a loss. K controls how fast ratings react: high K for short seasons (NFL), lower K for long ones (NBA, MLB).

Sports-specific tweaks

  • Home advantage: add a fixed bonus to the home side's rating before computing probability (often 50–100 points depending on sport).
  • Margin of victory: scale K by a function of the score margin so blowouts count more, with an autocorrelation adjustment so favourites are not over-rewarded.
  • Season reversion: pull ratings partway toward the mean between seasons to reflect roster turnover.

Strengths and limits

Elo needs only results, so it works in any sport with a schedule. It self-corrects and is easy to explain. Its limits: it does not know about injuries or trades until results arrive, and it treats every game as equally informative unless you adjust K. Good analysts combine Elo with player-level data for a fuller picture.
