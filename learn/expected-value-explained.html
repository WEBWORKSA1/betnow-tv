Expected Value (EV) Explained for Sports Fans

By the BetNow.tv Analytics Desk · 6 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

Expected value is the average result of a decision if you could repeat it thousands of times. It is the most important idea in any game of uncertainty, from poker theory to insurance pricing to fantasy sports roster decisions.

The formula

For a simple win/lose proposition at decimal odds d with your estimated probability p:

EV per unit = p × (d − 1) − (1 − p)

Example: you believe an outcome happens 45% of the time and it is priced at 2.40. EV = 0.45 × 1.40 − 0.55 = 0.63 − 0.55 = +0.08 per unit. On paper, that is a positive-expectation proposition.

Break-even probability

The break-even probability is just 1 ÷ d. At 2.40 it is 41.7%. If your honest estimate is above that, EV is positive; below, it is negative. The difference between your estimate and break-even is your "edge" in percentage points.

The catch: your probability must be right

EV is only as good as the probability you feed it. Most people overestimate their accuracy, especially on teams they support. That is why we publish model probabilities openly and track them — calibration (does a 60% forecast happen about 60% of the time?) is the honest test of any forecaster.

Variance: why good decisions still lose

A +8% EV proposition still loses 55% of the time in our example. Over 20 trials you can easily be behind. Over 2,000 trials, the average converges toward the expectation. This gap between short-run results and long-run expectation is called variance, and misunderstanding it is the root of most bad reasoning in sport — from "hot hands" to sacking a coach after three unlucky losses. Read more in Variance and Sample Size.

EV beyond wagering

EV thinking applies to free Pick'em contests, fantasy drafts (is the third running back worth more than the second receiver?), coaching decisions (fourth-down attempts in the NFL are a famous EV debate) and business.
